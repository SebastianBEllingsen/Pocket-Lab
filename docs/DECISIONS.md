### Decision record: how to pick the background colour (2026-10-11)
- Problem: the single "most common colour" rule fails when cells cover most of the frame (pale grey Data_Set_1 image 604478c7: IoU 0.37, 96% marked foreground).
- Options considered: (a) median filter of the image (`median`); (b) most common colour (`mode`); (c) let several independent proposers vote and take the colour most of them agree on (`consensus`, Sebastian's idea). Proposers: mode, bright (lightest large cluster), kmeans (lightest of 3 clusters), border (frame colour), neutral (least colourful large cluster).
- Pros/cons: median is simple but wrong with dense cells. Mode is simple and usually right, but has a clear failure case. Consensus is robust to any single proposer failing, and the votes are visible in the viewer; it costs about 5x the colour-finding time (small, done at 0.25 scale) and needs the proposers to fail in different ways. Ties or no agreement fall back to the first proposer and are flagged.
- Chosen: `consensus` as default. 
- Evidence (30 random Data_Set_1 images, `--random 30 --seed 5`): mean IoU median 0.789 (min 0.090), mode 0.864 (min 0.058), consensus 0.890 (min 0.753). Image 604478c7: 0.368 -> 0.904 (mode was outvoted 4 to 1). Reproduce: `python -m cellcount.bg_removal --method {median,mode,consensus} --random 30 --seed 5 --stats`.
- Remaining low scores (consensus, `--worst 5 --pool 150 --seed 1`: 0.46, 0.59, 0.71, 0.74, 0.74) are mostly label-policy questions rather than colour errors: a huge dark stain blob the reference mask leaves out (c6ab535c), and sparse pale/ghost cells, dust and a dark corner that we outline but the reference does not (ebb53337). To discuss with Sebastian.

### Decision record: threshold picker and eyepiece circle (2026-10-27, verdict pending)
- Problem (Sebastian saw it on BCCD/PBC/Data_Set_1): cells marked as open donuts, edges missed, some cells not marked. Cause: one global Otsu cut-off on the Lab distance map; a small dark region (WBC nucleus) pulls it up, so pale RBCs fall below it.
- Options (flag `--threshold`): `otsu` (old), `half` (0.5 x Otsu), `lower` (Otsu on the values below the first cut-off), `clipped` (Otsu after clipping at the median of the dark values). Several names after `--threshold` give a side-by-side comparison.
- Evidence: on PBC and BCCD images `half` and `lower` outline every RBC closed and filled, `otsu` gives donuts and misses. On Data_Set_1 (30 images, scratch test) mean IoU otsu 0.888, half 0.836, lower 0.790, because the reference mask omits ghosts and dust (label-policy gap, see above). On the own phone photos (cells faint, strong vignette) all pickers are poor: `otsu` marks mostly WBCs, `lower` marks the vignetted edge. Default stays `otsu` until Sebastian judges.
- Own photos: dark rim around a bright circle made the rim win the background vote. New `--fov auto` finds the circle (robust least-squares fit) and works inside it; the mask is cut to the circle, matching the detector's rim convention. Circle found on 60 of 63 photos at first, 62 of 63 after lowering the grey cut to 0.25 x Otsu; no false circles on 21 images each from Data_Set_1 and BCCD. Known issue: fitted circle bulges a few px on `unk_40x_06`.
- Consequence for the generator: the own photos have no cell-free glass, so backgrounds for them must come from the stage-1 background field estimate, not from cell-free patches.
- Reproduce: `python -m cellcount.bg_removal --dataset 3 --random 4 --threshold otsu,half,lower`; `python -m cellcount.bg_removal --image Data_Sets/own_v2/own_v2/unk_100x_05.jpg --threshold otsu,half,lower`.

### Decision record: build our own compositing, not a framework (2026-10-27)
- Checked (web search 2026-10-27, snippets only, not the repositories): Albumentations moved to AlbumentationsX under AGPL-3.0 or a commercial licence; the old MIT package is described by the vendor as frozen (no security patches). Ultralytics code, including its copy-paste augmentation, is AGPL-3.0. Neither fits our need anyway: we need optical-density blending, exact per-class labels and WBC subtype sidecars. Copy-paste in those libraries is a training-time augmentation, not a dataset generator.
- Literature: "Cut, Paste and Learn" (Dwibedi et al., ICCV 2017) found that combining blend modes beat Poisson blending alone (mAP 72.4 combined vs 58.4 Poisson vs 65.9 no blending on GMU) and that boundary artefacts matter; Dvornik et al. found random placement without context can hurt. Brunggel, Vallotton, Conway (SciTePress 2022) built artificial RBC overlaps from isolated cells for segmentation, the closest precedent for our overlap idea. Bailo et al. (CVPRW 2019) used conditional GANs for RBC images (generative alternative, not pursued: needs data and gives no exact labels).
- Chosen: own generator (`cellcount/synth/`), physical optical-density blending (overlaps darken), soft feathered alpha, blur plus noise plus JPEG after compositing (hides paste seams, per the literature), glass background from stage 1's estimated field (own photos have no cell-free glass). Pros: exact boxes and counts, controllable class ratios and overlap, licence-free. Cons: more code to maintain, realism judged by eye until the teammate's evaluation.
- Not yet tried: Poisson blending (literature says not better alone), GAN or diffusion generation, SAM for cut-outs.

### Decision record: cut-out sources and scale (2026-10-27, bank reviewed by contact sheets)
- RBC: Data_Set_1 isolated round blobs (scaled by each image's own median radius, about 50 px) and PBC neighbour cells (global radius 36 px, measured on 25 images). PBC cells have the pale centre the real photos show; Data_Set_1 cells are flat purple. A scene uses one source and always PBC's stain colour direction, because Data_Set_1's grey-blue stain did not match the photos.
- WBC and platelet: PBC. WBC cut with a two-step mask: strict `otsu` finds the dark core, then it is grown into the cytoplasm inside the wide `lower` mask until the shape stops being compact, then opened with a disc of 40% of the cell diameter to trim touching red cells. Keep rate 85-98% per WBC type, 66% platelets (first try with `lower` alone merged WBCs with neighbouring red cells; with `otsu` alone neutrophils lost their cytoplasm). A few cut-outs still carry scraps of red cells (known issue).
- Platelets: PBC's platelet class shows large thrombocytes, so they are rescaled to 0.2-0.45 of the red-cell radius to match "small purple dot".
- Smudges: procedural pale ragged purple blobs (no public source).
- Red-cell density is normalised per scene (mean OD 0.10-0.30) because stain density differs a lot between source images (first scenes had near-black cells next to pale ones).
- Teammate's conventions (2026-10-27, relayed by Sebastian): boxes tight around the whole outline (RBC with pallor; WBC with cytoplasm; smudge = whole pale patch; platelet = the dot), one box per cell also in clumps. Counting circle = 93% of eyepiece radius minus one red-cell radius; a cell counts when its box centre is inside. Training images are black outside the counting circle and cells there are unlabelled; cells whose centre is inside keep their full box even where the circle cuts them. The generator follows this (`rim_prob`, `rim_radius`, `count_frac`). Still open: his hand labels show red-cell boxes of about 23 px on a 240 px crop although radius 16 implies 32 px (he did not answer; `--rbc-radius` is a flag).
- Layout (Sebastian: scenes looked too clustered): red cells are arranged by one of three layouts per scene: random (dart throwing, weight 0.4), packed (jittered hexagonal grid, the 100x look, 0.25) or clumps (Gaussian clusters, the 40x look, 0.35); coverage 0.15-0.65, centre distance 0.7-1.05 diameters.
